## The tension

Blinkit has won the replenishment habit, and that same habit may limit category expansion. The better it gets at helping people **retrieve** what they already need, the less reason they have to **explore**.

<Compare
  beforeLabel="Familiar purchase: low effort"

  afterLabel="New category: high effort"

<Callout tone="question" label="Business objective">
  Increase the % of monthly active customers who buy from at least one new category every month.
</Callout>

The target segment: **high-frequency customers with narrow baskets**. They already trust the platform and order often, but their purchases stay in familiar aisles.

## Evidence, not assumptions

Instead of guessing where category expansion breaks, I combined public customer conversations, AI-assisted retrieval, structured behavioural tagging and human synthesis.

The classifier agreed with 50 human labels 92% of the time (Cohen's κ = 0.89). The schema allows "unknown" when evidence is thin. Code is on [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/snigdhanagpal97/blinkit-category-discovery-engine).

## What the reviews showed

Only ~1.2% of conversations touch category discovery, but that thin signal is strikingly consistent. It lines up with three barriers: **know** ("I didn't know they sold this"), **consider** ("I know, but it's not where I'd buy it") and **confidence** ("I can see it, but I can't choose confidently").

## The problem

High-frequency Blinkit customers order 4+ times a week, but their baskets stay in one or two categories. The moment a purchase needs reviews, comparison or return assurance, Blinkit isn't in the consideration set. It owns the urgent, low-decision trip, but it isn't yet the platform customers consider when a purchase takes thought.

## Three options

<Callout tone="question" label="How might we">
  Make Blinkit the platform customers consider for higher-consideration needs, without asking them to browse, compare or think in categories?
</Callout>

<Cards
  items={[
    { tag: "Option 1", title: "Awareness campaigns", body: "Push category ads. Solves know, but not consider: 3 of 6 already knew and still opened Amazon." },
    { tag: "Option 2", title: "Discovery rails", body: "Top picks and an AI next-product rail. Only reaches people already in the app, before consideration." },
    { tag: "Option 3 · Recommended", title: "Flash", body: "A situation-first AI layer. Describe the moment in one line and get a ready basket. Solves know, consider and confidence, with the highest build effort." },
  ]}
/>

Nobody shops for a category; they shop for a situation. For products, Amazon is the default. For situations, there's no default yet, and that's the consideration set Blinkit can own.

- **No searching.** Describe a situation instead of hunting for products one by one.
- **No decision fatigue.** At most one clarifying question, then a complete basket.
- **No forgotten items.** Flash remembers what the situation needs.

Flash understands Hinglish too: "bacche ko bukhar hai", "my kid is burning up" and "fever 102 what do I do" all lead to the same fever kit. A child's fever leads to pharmacy, a new puppy to pet care, an exam tomorrow to stationery. **The customer never picks a new category. The category comes with the solution.**
