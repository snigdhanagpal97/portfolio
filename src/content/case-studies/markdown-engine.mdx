## What 12 AM looked like before

Every night, low keep-life SKUs reached expiry and were disposed of as a total loss. No secondary channel, no recovery. The loss was a daily certainty, and it grew with every forecast error that caused an over-order earlier in the day.

- No automated trigger of any kind
- Manual intervention per store, per SKU, every night
- Expiring SKUs disposed of as a total loss
- Disposal costs reaching double digits as a percentage of fresh GMV
- Loss compounding with every over-order
- No way to recover category margin

<Callout tone="question" label="The goal">
  Find near-expiry stock automatically, boost its visibility to pull in demand, and re-price the rest to clear it, without eroding category margin.
</Callout>

## Four levers, every evening

Each module makes one decision. Together they run on the clock from 6 PM to midnight.

## 6 PM: visibility before price

At-risk SKUs move up in search and listing pages at 6 PM. There's no price change and no customer-facing markdown, so organic demand gets a two-hour window to absorb the excess at full margin.

## 8 PM: what to act on

When the boost window closes, every SKU × store pair is checked against its live inventory signal. Stock tracking below expected sales gets a small price uplift. Stock in excess gets a markdown. Both run at the same time but aren't symmetric: the uplift stays conservative because a price rise can kill conversion, and the markdown goes deeper because clearing expiring stock comes first.

### How fresh produce is tracked without an expiry date

Fresh produce has no expiry printed on the pack, so the system tracks each inbound batch by date. At 8 PM, only units from batches that hit their keep life today are flagged. Newer batches stay in normal stock.

### How much to clear

<Formula>Liquidation qty = Expiring stock − Expected natural sales</Formula>

- **Expiring stock:** all units of a SKU × store whose last sellable day is today.
- **Expected natural sales:** what the store is expected to sell at full price before close.
- **Liquidation qty:** what's left, which the markdown has to move.

## Markdown depth, hour by hour

A price elasticity model sets the depth needed to move the target quantity. The bigger the excess relative to expected sell-through, the deeper the markdown. It's recalculated every hour until midnight.

## Guardrails

Automation only earns trust when people can override it cleanly. Four guardrails wrap the whole pipeline, so category managers stay in control.

## A separate expected-sale model

<Compare
  beforeLabel="The risk"
  before={<>

  </>}
  afterLabel="What we built"
  after={<>
    <p><strong>An independent intraday expected-sale model.</strong></p>

  </>}
/>

Only a partial share of liquidation units feeds back into forward demand forecasts, so the system never starts forecasting for markdown sales. As accuracy improves, that share is designed to shrink automatically.

## Results

Launched in early 2025 with fruits and vegetables, then expanded to bakery, dairy, milk, meat and seafood, ready-to-eat, deli and flowers. Around 70% of the disposal improvement is attributed to this system, alongside gains in forecasting accuracy.
