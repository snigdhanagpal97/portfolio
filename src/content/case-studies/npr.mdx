## The problem

Blinkit's Next Product Recommendation (NPR) rail is the strip of suggestions that appears after a customer adds something to cart. It already existed, but it ranked items by overall popularity, often from the same product type.

<Callout tone="question" label="How might we">
  Recommend the right next product? One that fits the cart, reflects personal history, and actually gets added.
</Callout>

## Why it was harder than it looked

This was not just a ranking problem. The rail had to balance what felt like a natural next add, the right price point for an impulse buy, live inventory at the store, support for niche categories, and the business goal of reducing perishable disposal.

## The scoring system

For each cart state, the system shortlisted candidates, scored them, then filtered and ranked them before serving.

<Figure
  src="/images/case-studies/npr/03.png"
  width={1370}
  height={688}
  alt="Score weights: 50% cart affinity, 30% personalisation, 20% subtype affinity"
/>

<Formula>Final score = 0.5 × PMI basket + 0.3 × personalisation + 0.2 × subtype affinity</Formula>

**Why these weights:**

- **Cart context mattered most**, because the rail had to respond to what was already in the basket.
- **Personalisation helped** when history was available.
- **Subtype affinity was the fallback**, and supported broader category pairings.

## Why PMI, not raw counts

Every SKU was scored against every other SKU by how often they appeared in the same cart. Raw co-occurrence has a bias: items like milk and eggs are in so many carts that they top every list. PMI corrects for that.

## Personas instead of individuals

Individual history is thin in quick commerce. Most users order 2 to 4 times a month, so 90 days gives only 8 to 12 orders. Instead, users were clustered into behavioural personas, and each persona gave a dense, reliable signal. A user can belong to several personas, and recommendations combine all of them.

<Figure
  src="/images/case-studies/npr/05.png"
  width={2100}
  height={1300}
  alt="Persona network: parent, self care shopper, fitness enthusiast, bougie buyer, devotional buyer, snackster, beverage lover, home chef"
  wide
/>

<Callout label="Cold start">
  New users get a persona after their first 2 to 3 orders. Until then, the 30% personalisation weight moves to subtype affinity (50% PMI, 50% subtype), so they still get strong cross-category picks instead of generic bestsellers.
</Callout>

## Filters before serving

Scoring ranks the candidates. Filters decide what actually gets served, and they run in order.

  items={[
