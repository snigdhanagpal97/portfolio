## Wide reach, shallow engagement

Every day, over 100 million Indians open Spotify and stream for hours. But the experience is passive. Nothing turns that listening into something that feels like progress, and nothing makes leaving cost anything.

On price and catalogue alone, Spotify is one tap away from JioSaavn, Gaana or Wynk. Retention looks healthy. It isn't defensible.

- No structure on what people already stream
- No sense of progress; sessions blur together
- Switching cost to a rival is near zero
- Learning intent leaks to YouTube and Duolingo
- Retention is high but undifferentiated

## Who we're solving for

18 to 30 year olds who already use audio to learn, like streaming English music for vocabulary, then leave for YouTube and Duolingo the moment they want structure.

With 3 weeks and no engineering, I scored every feature on value versus effort and kept one rule: ship only what tests the core hypothesis, that a structured audio track feels like skill-building and users notice their progress.

## Measuring learning, not listening

The trap: minutes streamed is the entertainment metric this feature is trying to move past, so it can't be the success measure. The metric tree is deliberately narrow, and every target is a projection for the 1,000-user pilot.

<Steps
  vertical
  items={[
    { title: "North star", body: "Weekly structured-track completions per active user." },
    { title: "Activation", tag: "Target 60%", body: "% who set a goal." },

<Callout>The switching-cost edge holds only if the habit and perceived-learning numbers hold.</Callout>

## What I'd build next

- **Quizzes**, to validate learning beyond self-report.
- **Real personalisation**, once there's completion data to train on.
- **Badges**, to reinforce streaks and social proof.
- **Then the creator marketplace**, the supply-side bet.
